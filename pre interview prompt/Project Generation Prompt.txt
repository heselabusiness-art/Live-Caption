Project: Enterprise Agentic LLM Platform — Healthcare SaaS End-Client

Background and Context
I was placed by Valere, my consulting employer, with a healthcare SaaS company that built digital health products serving the insurance and clinical documentation space.
This company's platform sat at the intersection of clinical data and insurance workflows — it bridged the gap between EHR systems where clinical data lives and the insurance claims process that depends on that data being complete and accurate.
The end users were insurance agents and claims reviewers — people whose job is to decide whether a submitted claim is supported by sufficient clinical documentation.
Their decisions directly determine whether a claim gets approved, flagged, or denied.

Why This Project Was Required
The client was facing a serious, growing operational problem upstream of the claims process.
Healthcare data is fragmented — patient records, clinical notes, diagnostic codes, and physician documentation are scattered across three or four EHR systems per patient, each with its own format and completeness standards.
Before this project, reviewers did all of this manually — logging into multiple systems, reading notes, cross-referencing against the claim, and deciding.
It was slow, error-prone, and didn't scale.
As claim volumes grew, the bottleneck got worse, and even experienced reviewers made documentation errors under pressure.
The result was a high rate of claim denials driven not by invalid claims but by incomplete documentation — and those denials were costly and entirely preventable.

What We Were Asked to Build
Valere was engaged to design and deploy an enterprise agentic LLM platform that could autonomously handle the documentation review reviewers were doing by hand.
The agents had to read clinical notes across multiple EHR systems, reconcile the data, flag gaps, and deliver a structured validated summary — or in straightforward cases resolve the check end-to-end.
As the Principal AI Engineer, I was the architect responsible for the full platform design and delivery.

The Core Technical Challenges
The first challenge was scale and concurrency.
The platform processed tens of thousands of agent requests per day, each running multi-step reasoning across multiple EHR sources.
LLM inference is slow and expensive, so I made the call to go fully async and queue-driven on Vertex AI and Kubernetes — even though it added real architectural complexity — because a synchronous approach would collapse under production load.
We scaled concurrent agent execution capacity by 42% while keeping production reliability intact.

The second challenge was the messiness of real clinical data.
Notes are free-form, codes vary in specificity, fields go missing, and the same patient appears under different identifiers across systems.
I decided against a single general-purpose agent — it would've been faster to build, but I saw firsthand it collapsed on edge cases.
I chose a multi-agent architecture on LangGraph with a routing layer I designed to classify each request and dispatch it to specialized sub-agents, with evaluation checkpoints between stages.
This reduced manual review requirements by 37% in production.

The third challenge was reliability and auditability in a regulated environment.
An agent surfacing wrong data or triggering a wrongful denial carries real consequences, so I held a much higher reliability bar than a consumer product would.
I built output validation layers, guardrails, structured schemas, and behavioral evaluation frameworks that tested consistency without needing labeled ground truth for every case.
I also designed human-in-the-loop escalation for high-stakes cases.
These improved behavioral consistency 31% and cut incorrect autonomous actions 29%.

The fourth challenge was governance.
Every action had to be logged and explainable, and I treated prompt versioning and regression testing as first-class — because I'd seen silent model drift in production and wouldn't let it happen here.
I built a full LLMOps governance stack — drift detection, evaluation pipelines, audit trails, prompt versioning — cutting production model incidents 33%.

The Business Impact
The platform changed how the claims operation worked — reviewers stopped manually piecing together documentation and instead reviewed agent-prepared summaries.
Agent task completion accuracy improved 38%.
Benchmark performance on customer-facing agent workloads improved 27% through prompt optimization.
Model evaluation failures across the engineering org dropped 34%.
And the LLMOps governance standards I built became the baseline the broader org adopted.
