# COMPANION 2: INTERVIEW LOOP FACT LOCK

Fills **LOCKED FROM MY INTERVIEW LOOP** in the onboarding assistant prompt, including the why-I-left line and the scripted self intro. Run once, before week one.

This is the highest risk block in the whole system. Rule 4 of the assistant depends entirely on it. A number here that does not match what I said in the loop is a contradiction the same people will hear.

This is a normal analysis tool, not a live speaking prompt.

═══════════════════════════════════════════

You are helping me lock the facts I stated during my interview loop so I never contradict myself after starting. Your only job is to produce a paste ready block.

## WHAT I WILL GIVE YOU

- my interview assistant materials block, the one with my anchor project and exact numbers
- any interview transcripts, recordings notes, or my own scribbles from the rounds
- my resume as submitted
- anything I told the recruiter about why I was leaving

## WHAT TO DO

**Step one. Extract every factual claim I made about my own work.** Numbers, scale, team size, timelines, technologies, outcomes. Build a complete list before you write anything.

**Step two. Classify every claim into one of three buckets.**

- **STATED.** It appears in a transcript, in my materials block, or on my resume. Safe.
- **PROBABLY STATED.** It is in my materials but I have no evidence I actually said it in a round. Usable, but flag it.
- **UNCERTAIN.** Two sources disagree, or a number appears in more than one form. **Default to the most conservative version, always.** Understating something I overstated is survivable. The reverse is not.

**Step three. Extract the gaps I openly admitted.** Every "I haven't worked with that directly" or "we never got to that." These matter as much as the claims. If I suddenly demonstrate deep expertise in something I said I lacked, people notice, and it retroactively makes every other claim suspect. Capture them precisely.

**Step four. Write the why-I-left sentence.** One sentence. Forward looking, about what I am moving toward, never about what I was escaping. Never negative about the previous employer, a client, a manager, or a person. It must be consistent with whatever I told the recruiter. If I gave you that, match it closely. If I did not, ask me for it before writing. This gets asked in every peer coffee chat.

**Step five. Write the 40 second self intro.** I will deliver this fifteen times in the first two weeks and it must be identical every time. Structure: where I came from at a high level, what I am here to work on, one human detail. **No metrics, no project story, no credentials pile.** The human detail is not optional. It is what makes people remember me rather than my resume. Ask me for it if you do not have one.

Write the intro in delivery format: one sentence per line, one or two bold stress words per sentence, a "/" at each natural pause and a final "/" at the stop. Around 90 to 110 words.

**Step six. Ask me at most three questions**, only where the answer changes what gets locked.

## OUTPUT FORMAT

Produce exactly this, nothing before, nothing after.

```
**LOCKED FROM MY INTERVIEW LOOP**
- Anchor project and metrics: [exact numbers, what each measured]
- Scale facts: [team size, volume, model count]
- Gaps I openly named in the loop: [the honest nots]
- Why I left, exactly as I said it in the loop: [one sentence]
- My 40 second self intro, scripted:
[the intro, in delivery format]
```

Then, under a heading `UNCERTAIN, DO NOT SAY`, list every claim that landed in the uncertain bucket, with the conservative version I should use if it comes up and the aggressive version I must avoid. This list is as important as the block itself.

Then, under `PROTECTED`, list every previous client name, proprietary system name, and internal metric that appeared in my materials but must never be spoken at the new company. Be thorough here. I will read this before my first week.

## HARD RULES

- Never round a number up. Never smooth a range into its best end.
- Never invent a metric that is not in my sources, even if it would obviously exist.
- If a claim appears in my resume but nowhere in the loop, mark it PROBABLY STATED rather than STATED.
- Do not tell me my claims sound impressive or weak. That is not the job here.
